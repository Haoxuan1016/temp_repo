Question 1 · Definition & calculation

Utility is satisfaction. We measure it in imaginary units: utils.

Total utility (TU) — the total satisfaction from consuming a given quantity of a good in a time period.

Marginal utility (MU) — the extra satisfaction from consuming one more unit.

MU = ΔTUΔQ and TU = ΣMU

So MU is the slope of the TU curve, and TU is the running total (area) of the MU column. Never mix them up in an exam.

Question 2 · The law that drives everything

The law of diminishing marginal utility

As more units of a good are consumed within a given time period, the marginal utility gained from each successive unit falls.

Why? Each cup satisfies a less urgent want than the one before. Cup 1 wakes you up; cup 4 just sloshes. Wants get progressively satisfied, so intensity of desire falls.

Three checkpoints
· MU falling but positive: TU still rising, just more slowly
· MU = 0: TU at its maximum (satiation, cup 5)
· MU negative: TU falls (disutility — you feel sick)

Hover a bar to read its MU.
Say it: “This is the engine of the whole topic. Because MU falls, the consumer will only take extra units if they’re asked to give up less in return — i.e. if the price is lower.”

Question 3 · Spending a limited budget

The equi-marginal principle

Utility is maximised when the last dollar spent on every good yields the same marginal utility:

MUAPA = MUBPB = … = MUNPN

We divide by price because goods cost different amounts — MU alone isn’t comparable. MU/P is “utils per dollar”, or value for money.

If MUA/PA > MUB/PB, good A is better value, so the consumer reallocates spending towards A. Buying more of A pushes MUA down; buying less of B pushes MUB up — until the ratios are equal. That is consumer equilibrium.

Question 4 · The pay-off

Deriving the individual demand curve

In equilibrium the consumer equates value for money across every use of income:

MUcoffeePcoffee = MU of money = 6 utils per $

Rearranged: MU = 6 × P. Buy every cup that clears the bar.

  1. Price falls, so MU/P rises above 6: better value.
  2. Equilibrium disturbed — spending switches into coffee.
  3. Extra cups have lower MU, so MU/P falls back to 6.
  4. New equilibrium at higher Q. Plot P against Q: demand.

The answer · and where it breaks down

A price cut raises utils per dollar. Diminishing MU decides how many extra units it takes to restore equilibrium.

Strength

Explains the law of demand from consumer behaviour, and predicts how much more is bought — not just the direction.

Limitation

Utility is cardinal here: it assumes satisfaction is measurable and comparable. In reality it is only ordinal — we can rank, not count.

Limitation

Assumes rational, fully-informed consumers with constant tastes and MU of money. Behavioural economics — habit, bounded rationality, conspicuous consumption — challenges this.

Exam link: indifference curve analysis avoids measuring utility and splits the response to a price change into substitution and income effects — the same conclusion, weaker assumptions.
